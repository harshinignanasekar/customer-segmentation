The question
Starting point
Not all customers shop the same way. So why treat them like they do?
The Instacart dataset captures 3.4 million grocery orders across 206,000 customers. On the surface it looks like one large customer base. But underneath, customers behave very differently. Some shop every week without fail, some browse broadly and occasionally, and some place one big order then go quiet for weeks. This analysis uses K-Means clustering on 7 behavioural features (order frequency, basket size, reorder rate, department breadth, unique products, shopping cadence and order history) to reveal three genuinely distinct customer personalities.

What the data showed
The finding
Three distinct shopping personalities emerged from the data
Running K-Means across the 7 features, the silhouette score peaked at k=3, meaning three clusters are the most natural grouping in this dataset. The segments are not arbitrary slices; they reflect genuinely different shopping personalities. A smaller group of highly loyal, high-frequency shoppers who order every 8 days. A large middle group of engaged but irregular buyers exploring broadly. And a second large group who shop infrequently but spend big when they do.

The surprising insights
Counterintuitive finding
Infrequent Buyers have the biggest baskets
At first glance, Infrequent Buyers look like the lowest-value group. They shop rarely, averaging just 7.9 orders and 19.6 days between visits. But their basket size tells a different story: 14.8 items per order, the highest of any segment. These customers don't shop often, but when they do, they shop big. This suggests they consolidate their grocery run into occasional large purchases rather than frequent top-ups. A behaviour worth leaning into with bulk-buy promotions and free delivery thresholds rather than trying to increase visit frequency.
The biggest opportunity
Casual Explorers are the largest untapped segment
Casual Explorers make up 41.5% of the customer base, the single largest group. They're already engaged (22.4 orders on average, 57% reorder rate) but haven't yet built the strong habits of Habitual Reorderers. They're the middle of the funnel: active enough to be worth investing in, not yet loyal enough to be retained passively. Moving even 10% of Casual Explorers into Habitual Reorderer behaviour would represent a significant commercial uplift. Achievable through personalised product recommendations, subscription nudges and targeted promotions on their top categories.
What to do about it. Recommendations by segment
Habitual Reorderers
  • Subscribe and save. Auto-replenishment fits their weekly cadence perfectly
  • Introduce adjacent products within top departments (produce → organic range)
  • Loyalty rewards and early access to new products. Protect this group above all
  • Cross-sell into unexplored departments. They have highest breadth potential
Casual Explorers
  • Largest segment (41.5%), the biggest commercial opportunity in aggregate
  • Personalised recommendations based on their broad browsing and purchase history
  • Incentivise the 3rd and 4th order. Habit forms after repeated positive experience
  • Curated bundles and seasonal campaigns to build frequency over time
Infrequent Buyers
  • Large basket (14.8 items), high intent when they shop. Lean into it
  • Bulk-buy promotions and free delivery above a spend threshold
  • Time-sensitive win-back campaigns when gaps exceed 30 days
  • Set a 90-day reactivation window. Deprioritise if no response
